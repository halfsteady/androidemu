A

Four Screens

a 2x2 checker of bright cells with a small sprite in each

The brief, taken literally: the square cut in half twice, a different game in each quarter. Two of Emulia's accents alternate so every cell stays bright and the checker is what survives.

Trade-off
Measured: each sprite pixel is 1.19px at mdpi. The four sprites stop being four games and become texture; what is left is the checker, which no longer says NES.

B

The Cabinet

a small dark CRT with four glowing sprites on it

The same four games stated as a screen instead of a chart -- phosphor glow, a glass bezel, scanlines. Emulia ships ten picture modes; this is the icon that admits the app is about a display.

Trade-off
Same 1.19px sprites as (a), and the scanlines are a 1.15dp feature that is gone below 96px. Dark ground with bright specks is also the shape most likely to read as a generic emulator icon.

C

One in Four

one big 8-bit bug on four blocks of colour

Still four quarters, but the variety moved to the ground and one sprite lies across all of it. It is what the app actually does: the same game, every palette, and a .pal importer to add more.

Trade-off
The only quadrant concept whose pixels are still pixels at 48px (2.86px each). Cost is that two of the four cells leave Emulia's greens, so the mark owns its shape more than it owns its colour. Its counters also stay open all the way down (alpha 0 at 48px, 9 at 32px, against 157 for A and B) -- the eyes are still eyes at 32px.

D

The Emu

a chunky pixel emu, side on, in Emulia's green

Emu-lia. The name has a bird in it and has had one all along; the extra syllables were always doing that. A flightless bird for an app that runs games that cannot run anywhere else.

Trade-off
Nothing in it says NES, or emulator, or games -- it is a bird until somebody tells you the joke. And at 1.60px per pixel it is the LEGS that go first, not the neck: the 3.50dp gap between them fills to alpha 161/255 at 48px, so the bird stands on a wedge. The neck is three pixels wide and safe.

E

Half and Half

an 8-bit bug whose right half has melted

One sprite drawn twice: hard pixels on the left, the same silhouette rounded and merged on the right. Emulia's picture settings are an argument about how a pixel should be drawn, and this is that argument as a shape.

Trade-off
Reads as a rendering bug rather than a rendering choice until it is explained, and the joke needs both halves -- so it is the concept that loses the most to a circle mask. The join is a tonal step in the ground, not a line: a 1.2dp seam measured 0.53px at mdpi and smeared into a crack, so it was removed. Measured separately: the smoothing eats its own counters -- growing the rounded runs to merge them narrows the eye gaps from 6.07dp to 3.25dp, so E's counters close sooner than C's despite the same sprite at nearly the same size.

How one of these ships

All five are built and measured against the same six checks — nothing here is a mockup, and every raster on this page is the file that would ship. Nothing is chosen yet, so apply.py is deliberately not ported from the sibling project: the promotion step gets written once there is something to promote, against the one concept that won rather than against five that might.

# edit the pixel maps, then regenerate the three SVG layers
python3 brand/tools/pixels.py

# rebuild every concept from its three SVG layers, then re-measure
python3 brand/tools/build.py
python3 brand/tools/validate.py
python3 brand/tools/contact_sheet.py
